My Story

Curiosity, formalized.

The path from curiosity to CTF wins, and where I'm headed next.

Bio

Blessing Ndoro is a Cybersecurity student at Southeast Missouri State University with a focus on digital forensics and incident response. A first-place solo Capture the Flag winner and a selected BETA tester for World Wide Technology's CTF challenges, Blessing pairs hands-on offensive thinking with the patience of a forensic investigator — piecing together the story behind a breach rather than just patching the hole.

Outside the terminal, Blessing serves as President of the SEMO Chess Club and as a Senior Ambassador for the Office of Admissions, and has applied that same strategic thinking to hackathon work, building an AI-powered fall-detection and care platform from scratch. Blessing is currently working toward CompTIA Security+ and CHFI certifications, sharpening a focus on forensics-driven security work.

My Journey

Fall 2023 — Present

The Beginning: SEMO & a Calling Toward Cybersecurity

I started at Southeast Missouri State University in Fall 2023, drawn to cybersecurity by one simple thing: curiosity. In a world where threats evolve daily, I wanted a skill set that would constantly challenge me to think like an attacker and stay one step ahead. It felt less like choosing a major and more like answering a calling.

Oct 2023 — Present

Leading On and Off the Board

Alongside coursework, I took on leadership roles that mirror the strategic thinking cybersecurity demands. As President of the Chess Club, I get to sharpen the same pattern-recognition and long-range planning I use in security work. As a Senior Ambassador for the Office of Admissions, I help guide the next generation of SEMO students through their own beginnings.

2024 — Present

Testing Myself: CTFs and Real Competition

Coursework only goes so far, so I started competing. I took first place solo in a Capture the Flag competition hosted by Cyber Pro AI, working through reverse-engineering challenges and scripting my way past a privilege-escalation puzzle most competitors couldn't crack. Since then I've kept competing — a 1st-place finish with a cash prize and a 4th-place solo run in an 8-hour Active Directory red-team CTF among them. Full write-ups are on the Projects page.

Summer 2025 (Jun — Aug)

Into the Field: Missouri Cybersecurity Center of Excellence

My first real taste of enterprise security work came as a Cybersecurity Intern with the Missouri Cybersecurity Center of Excellence. I helped assess a city's IT infrastructure — deploying SentinelOne and ConnectWise RMM, documenting assets, and running ransomware threat analysis. I also got hands-on with penetration testing, HTB labs, and SOC operations: log monitoring, threat detection, and incident response, all grounded in NIST, ISO 27001, and Cloud Control Matrix frameworks. Later, working with the Incident Response Team, I helped fully contain an active ransomware threat with 22 identified indicators — with no impact to normal operations.

Aug 2025 — Present

Building While Learning: IT Help Desk & AI

Since August 2025, I've worked as an IT Help Desk Technician at SEMO, troubleshooting real problems for real people across Windows, macOS, and mobile — a different kind of pressure than a CTF, but just as valuable. In parallel, I helped build a Life Care AI Assistant, an AI-powered platform addressing medication non-adherence among isolated elderly patients — leading systems integration across a 4-person team so the medication reminder, dashboards, fall detection, and caregiver-alert modules worked together as one unified platform.

Feb 2026 — Present

CTF Beta Tester — World Wide Technology

Remote. Beta testing WWT's "Redhawk Recon" Capture the Flag challenge, built on Zscaler's cloud security platform (ZIA, ZPA, and Data Protection) — evaluating the realism and difficulty of the simulated environment. I investigated a simulated APT breach targeting a healthcare organization, tracing phishing lures, command-and-control traffic, SQL injection attempts, and data exfiltration using Zscaler tooling, Wireshark, and Ghidra. Working within a 3-4 person team under a 4-hour time limit, I helped identify and defend compromised systems, then delivered feedback on scenario design, accuracy, and pacing to help refine the CTF.

Feb 2026 — May 2026

Widening the Field: Healthcare IT

I took on a Desktop Support Intern role at Saint Francis Healthcare System, resolving daily tickets alongside a senior technician — printers, thin clients, laptops, and troubleshooting stuck Epic EHR sessions. I also supported the networking team with firewall and router management, imaged and decommissioned devices across the hospital network, and applied Duo two-factor authentication practices. It was a crash course in supporting a network where downtime has real consequences.

Jun 2026 — Jul 2026

Into the Enterprise: Banking IT

I spent early summer as an IT Intern at The Bank of Missouri, provisioning servers, PCs, and thin clients for new-employee and branch rollouts. I audited over 400 devices across branches in Clayton, St. Louis, and Springfield — completing one 300-device audit in just three days — and patched 80+ devices on a biweekly cadence to stay compliant, including remediating CVE-2026-41089, a Windows NetLogon flaw.

Present — Future

Where I'm Headed Next

I'm currently working toward my CompTIA Security+ and CHFI certifications. This past year has taken me from a hospital network to a bank's IT infrastructure to half a dozen competitive CTFs — each one sharpening the same instinct: piece together what happened, then make sure it doesn't happen again. I'm looking for a role where I can bring that mindset into a real SOC or forensics team, and keep building from there.
